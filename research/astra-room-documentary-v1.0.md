# Room for Revision

## A 25-minute documentary proposal for OpenAI

**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** Proposal and production plan. The room has not been built. Field survey, trade quotes, orders, construction, commissioning, final performance, invoices, and the finished film are pending. No outside film spend is authorized.  
**Prepared for:** OpenAI and The Recording Club  
**Public project:** [Rehearsal Room Build Guide](https://therecordingclub.github.io/rehearsal-build-public/)

## 1. The proposal at a glance

Working musician and studio owner Greg Spero began without design or construction experience, but knew how musicians use the room and when an image felt wrong. Through corrections, he used Astra to turn an approved visual concept into an inspectable room plan: recorded geometry, an editable 2D and 3D model, buyable furniture research, a line-item budget, trade scopes, material kits, installation steps, and release gates for facts that still need people on site.

The proposed film follows that work through the physical build. It begins in the real unfinished room and with the earliest recoverable prompt, watches a poor first model fail, stays present for measurement corrections and budget pressure, and continues through trade validation, procurement, construction, commissioning, a controlled musical performance, and the actual-invoice postmortem.

**Format:** 25-minute observational documentary, 16:9 master, clean master, full captions, transcript, trailer, cutdowns, stills, and a public source appendix.

**Proposed ask:** OpenAI evaluates whether to follow or develop the story and identifies an editorial contact for a first conversation. If there is interest, TRC and a production partner would agree on access, production scope, editorial roles, distribution, and a quoted full-film budget. Production resources or support are an optional discussion, not a request approved by this document. This proposal does not claim OpenAI participation, funding, or endorsement.

The room’s hard **$25,000 project cap is separate**. The current price-revision 1.7 room options are $24,948.52 balanced, $24,953.78 ceiling-first, and $23,257.16 with more cost protection. Each includes a $1,000 evidence-capture allowance for an archive and rough assembly. The finished 25-minute documentary needs a separate production agreement and quoted budget.

## 2. Why this story belongs at OpenAI

The subject is not a clean demo. The camera sees where an agent helps and where it stops being authoritative.

- Astra can organize records, compare sources, research products, debug geometry and code, generate or revise planning images, assemble budgets, and keep unresolved facts visible.
- Greg decides the room’s purpose, taste, acceptable compromises, spending cap, and whether a result feels usable to musicians.
- Bounded GPT workers handle isolated research, testing, extraction, or review tasks. Astra integrates and judges that work.
- An earlier approved concept came from Greg’s prior room work with Claude. It remains the visual starting point and receives visible credit. The film does not recast that concept as Astra’s original creation.
- Qualified people measure the site, engineer or approve load and attachment details, quote work, fabricate, install, inspect, and commission the room. A model cannot certify a wall, circuit, overhead assembly, or field fit.

## 3. The film’s premise and dramatic question

**Logline:** A musician with no design or construction background pushes Astra past a bad first model and a beautiful but incomplete concept, then follows the resulting plan into a real $25,000 room build where measurements, trades, receipts, and music decide what survives.

**Dramatic question:** Can a persistent, domain-expert user and an AI agent carry a room from an image to a defensible plan, then let the physical build expose every assumption?

The stakes are specific. The room has a fixed cap. The first 3D pass is poor. The room shape and door assumptions change when old plans surface. The sofa direction changes after price, availability, shape, and fit review; a concept-faithful custom configuration still lacks a final dealer quote. Two raised storage-door platforms remain hidden in the available record and must be measured before fit can be released. The budget reaches within $51.48 of the cap in the balanced option. Any wrong dimension can move furniture, change quantities, or stop custom work.

The ending is conditional. If the room opens, the film compares the built result with the plan and plays music in it. If scope changes or part of the design is deferred, the film shows the change order and why. If the project pauses, the documentary ends honestly on the verified limit rather than manufacturing a reveal.

### Astra during construction: proposed future workflow

This has not happened yet.

1. The crew submits labeled photos, measurements, delivery records, and the condition found.
2. Astra compares them with the versioned plan, identifies affected scopes, and requests missing facts instead of guessing.
3. The qualified trade chooses and releases the physical method, attachment, circuit, fabrication detail, or repair.
4. Astra updates the drawing, quantity, order tracker, budget, dependency, and change log.
5. Greg approves the cost and design tradeoff within the $25,000 cap before work or purchase proceeds.
6. The installer completes the work; the crew captures the check; Astra logs the as-built result, invoice state, and remaining holds.

Possible scenes include a raised platform dimension changing a route or fit check; wall backing for five mirrors or the TV requiring a trade detail; a stock item becoming unavailable; or an on-site clearance missing the model. Capture them only if they occur.

## 8. Conflicts the camera can observe

The film does not need invented jeopardy. It already has unresolved choices and visible failure points.

- **The poor first 3D:** orientation, furniture scale, shapes, and room relationships are visibly weak. Put it beside the current room long enough to inspect.
- **Shape and dimension corrections:** the polygon, non-45-degree returns, doors, booth, and object axes change what fits. Animate the changed line and show the model update.
- **The sofa problem:** a concept-faithful custom solution carries uncertain price, lead time, and final configuration. Lower-cost candidates depart from the concept in curve, arms, upholstery, or size. Film the decision without calling a selected reference an order or a price unknown unaffordable.
- **The $25,000 cap:** the balanced package leaves $51.48 after current reserves; the ceiling-first package leaves $46.22; the reserve-protected package leaves $1,742.84. Quotes can force deferral.
- **Hidden platforms and field gaps:** both raised storage-door areas need width, depth, height, edge, and door-clearance measurements. Their absence is a stop sign, not a detail to smooth over.
- **Trade authority:** mirror retention, overhead support, fire performance, electrical compatibility, backing, and final attachment remain outside the model’s authority.
- **The invoice test:** the budget is a planning ledger. Only accepted quotes, purchase records, change orders, and paid invoices can establish actual cost.

## 9. Observational style

Use one locked 4K 16:9 camera position before any room change and recreate it after commissioning. Record the lens, height, distance, tilt, white balance, lighting state, and time-of-day window. Keep the before grade honest. Native screen recordings show the interface and source changes; cameras show Greg deciding, trades measuring, and the room changing.

Five labels prevent category mistakes: `RECORD`, `MODEL`, `VISUALIZATION`, `FIELD VERIFIED`, and `PENDING`. Every cost displays its version and status: observed price, allowance, accepted quote, purchase, or invoice. Tape pulls, laser beeps, keys, drum hits, saws, drivers, relay clicks, and room tone should shape the soundtrack. Capture 60 seconds of room tone at every major phase.

## 11. Interviews that produce evidence

**Greg, before build and after commissioning**

- What did you know about rehearsing and recording here that the first model could not know?
- What was visibly wrong with the first 3D pass?
- Which part of the approved concept were you least willing to lose?
- When did a source or measurement change your mind?
- Which item would you refuse to order before a field check?
- At the cap, what did you keep, defer, or simplify, and why?
- After the build, what did the model get right? What changed on site?
- Would you use this process again, and what would you do earlier?

**Lead builder or survey lead**

- Which guide pages helped you quote or sequence the work?
- Which assumptions failed at the tape measure?
- What still required a shop drawing, engineer, manufacturer instruction, or trade decision?
- Which mockup or hold point prevented rework?
- Where did the documentation create more work, and was that work useful?

**Glazier or millworker**

- What information did the image communicate well?
- Which dimensions, substrate facts, templates, loads, edge details, and access requirements controlled fabrication?
- What changed between the visual target and the released shop drawing?

**Electrician or systems installer**

- Which proposed lighting or power assumptions survived the site survey?
- What did circuit condition, driver location, dimming compatibility, service access, or code require?
- Which labels and commissioning checks will the studio keep using?

**Musicians using the completed room**

- Can you set up, see one another, reach instruments, and move through the room as intended?
- Which physical choice affects the session most?
- What still gets in the way?

Do not ask anyone to endorse Astra or OpenAI. Ask for examples, corrections, and limits. Generic praise is expendable.

## 13. Production phases, media control, and consent

**Phase A, now through field survey:** recover the earliest prompt trail; lock the story and source map; capture the untouched room, Greg’s first interview, native screens, and model versions; collect releases; photograph every surface and hidden-area access point.

**Phase B, trade development and procurement:** film M-01–M-16, mockups, samples, redlines, scope choices, bids, final product selections, quote revisions, and order releases. Dates follow the real project calendar rather than a date invented for the proposal.

**Phase C, construction:** schedule capture around irreversible milestones. The producer receives at least 48 hours’ notice when possible before demolition, closure, delivery, and commissioning. A missed irreversible shot is logged as missed; it is never reenacted without an explicit `RECONSTRUCTION` label.

**Phase D, closeout:** shoot matched final views, performance, interviews, punch list, as-built evidence, and the actual-invoice review. Lock the edit only after factual and rights checks.

Every card is copied to **two separate verified media backups** before reuse. Use `YYYYMMDD_PHASE_CAMERA_SHOT_TAKE.ext`, maintain a checksum or verified-copy log, and keep the shot/asset register current with: source, creator, capture date, consent, rights, privacy status, evidence status, edit use, and backup locations. Keep one backup physically separate from the working drive.

Obtain appearance releases for identifiable people and location permission for every work area. Redact private prompts, account data, contact details, addresses, serial numbers, payment data, unreleased prices, and sensitive construction/security information. Film readable screens natively when possible, then make a redacted review export. The final fact check includes a model-role audit so no narration or title card assigns Astra work to Claude, Claude concept work to Astra, helper work to a single model, or professional trade decisions to AI.
