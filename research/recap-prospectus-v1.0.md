# Recap video production prospectus v1.0

Status: production plan only. The physical room build, final filming, edit, and release have not happened.

Prepared for: The Recording Club

Working format: 5:30 documentary case study, plus 30 and 60 second cutdowns

## The film

### Recommended title

**Room for Revision**

Subtitle: **How Greg and Astra turned an approved room concept into a construction planning system**

### Alternate titles

1. **You Still Have to Do Better**
2. **The Room Was the Prompt**
3. **From Render to Receipts**
4. **Every Last Detail**
5. **Astra, Redesign the Room**

### Logline

A studio owner starting without design or construction experience pushes Astra through bad models, wrong assumptions, real records, exact products, and hundreds of build checks to redesign a working rehearsal room. The final chapter follows professionals as they verify and build it.

### Editorial thesis

The story lives in the corrections. Greg brings taste, persistence, and deep music and studio experience. Astra acts as the primary design and production agent, owning the research, modeling, procurement logic, and guide assembly. Bounded GPT helpers handle isolated tasks. Existing approved concept art from Greg's earlier room work supplies the visual starting point. The room records, trades, and physical site supply the facts that software cannot invent.

The film should leave creative studio owners thinking, "I could run a serious project this way if I stay responsible for judgment and proof." A broader AI audience should see the work between a prompt and a defensible plan.

### Audience

- Primary: creative studio owners, producers, musicians, and small business operators considering AI-assisted design or renovation.
- Secondary: general AI viewers who want a real workflow with visible mistakes, corrections, and limits.
- Tertiary: designers, builders, and technology teams interested in human-directed agent work.

### The claim we can support

Greg began this redesign without design or construction experience. He did not begin without musical, recording, or room-use experience. The project now has an approved visual direction, source-backed geometry records, editable 2D and 3D planning tools, product research, and a v1.5 installation guide with **37 scopes, 158 component or service lines, 227 ordered steps, and 112 acceptance checks**.

### The claim we cannot support yet

- The room has not been physically built.
- Field dimensions, both raised storage-door areas, and several clearances remain unverified.
- Custom mirror, millwork, electrical, acoustic, glazing, and attachment details still need qualified trade or engineering release.
- Orders, invoices, installed costs, schedule performance, realized savings, and final results do not exist yet.
- Final before-and-after photography and video must be captured after installation.
- v1.6 cost work is in progress. Its amounts and counts are not final and must stay out of narration until verified.

The current film cannot truthfully be called a beginning-to-end build recap. Production should start now, preserve the remaining process, and lock the final edit only after installation and reveal.

## Creative direction

**Design read:** a documentary case study for studio owners and a broad AI audience, using a dark TRC field-journal language with warm brass marks, contact sheets, timecode, red pencil corrections, and real room evidence.

**Direction name:** **The marked-up studio field journal**. It feels like an editor opened the room's working binder beside a mixing console at midnight.

**Design dials:** variance 7, motion 3, density 5. The film and prospectus should feel authored and tactile. Motion stays restrained so records, corrections, and images carry the story.

**Palette:** room black `#100d0a`, charcoal `#1d1915`, paper `#f1eadf`, brass `#c18a43`, quiet line `#41382d`, muted note `#aaa092`. Green, amber, and red appear only as evidence status.

**Type:** Barlow Condensed for documentary titles, IBM Plex Sans for reading, IBM Plex Mono for timecode, filenames, dimensions, and proof labels. Use the local project font files.

**Signature device:** every major sequence carries an evidence state in the picture: `record`, `model`, `visualization`, `field verified`, or `pending capture`. The labels prevent an AI render from reading as a completed room.

**Image rules:**

- `assets/current-room.jpeg` is the real room before construction.
- `assets/approved-concept.png` is the approved AI concept and design starting point.
- `evidence/planner-3d-export-v1.1.png` is an early planning model and should look rough.
- `assets/room-3d-v1.4.png` is the corrected live 3D model.
- `assets/room-photo-v1.4.png` is a finished visualization generated from the planned layout.
- The final installed room requires new photography and video. Do not substitute a visualization.

## Sample script

All lines below are proposed narration unless marked as a verbatim prompt.

### Hook, 00:00 to 00:30

**GREG VO:** I had never designed or built a room like this. I did know the room, and I knew when the picture in front of me was wrong.

**ON SCREEN, VERBATIM PROMPT:** “make the furniture accurately sized and shaped!!”

**GREG VO:** The first 3D pass was rough. The room was turned around, the furniture was the wrong scale, and a polished image could hide all of it.

**ON SCREEN, VERBATIM PROMPT:** “you still have to do better”

**GREG VO:** So I kept going. I made Astra find the records, correct the room, price real products, and write down how every part would get built and checked.

### Turn, around 03:05

**ON SCREEN, VERBATIM PROMPT:** “i mean EVERYTHING - all the walls mirrors desk every last detail of the room needs to be found and priced out”

**GREG VO:** That changed the job. A picture was no longer enough. Every object needed a source, every custom piece needed a hold point, and every unknown needed to stay visible.

### Close, 05:00 to 05:30

**GREG VO:** We have not built the room yet. Measurements, engineering, trade drawings, orders, invoices, installation, and the final reveal are still ahead.

**GREG VO:** What Astra helped me build first was a way to make the project inspectable. I can see the room, move it, trace each decision, and hand each trade a clear next step.

**GREG VO:** The finished room will be the last piece of evidence. We are filming that next.

**END CARD:** The Recording Club / Room for Revision / Follow the build

## Interview plan

### Greg, story owner

Capture before the build, after the field survey, and after reveal.

- What did you know about this room that no plan or model could tell Astra?
- What was wrong with the first 3D version?
- Why did you keep pushing after the approved concept already looked good?
- When did the project stop feeling like an image exercise?
- Which correction cost the most time, and which saved the most uncertainty?
- What does "no experience" mean here, and what experience did you bring?
- What would you refuse to order without a field check?
- After the reveal: what did the model get right, and what changed on site?

### Astra process voice

Use restrained on-screen excerpts or a recorded interface demonstration. Do not personify bounded helpers as separate creative owners.

- Show how the primary agent separated records, model assumptions, proposed dimensions, and future field facts.
- Show one mistake, its source correction, the updated model, and the downstream guide change.
- Show how bounded GPT helpers received isolated tasks and returned evidence to Astra for final integration.

### Lead builder or general contractor

Record after survey and near handoff.

- Which parts of the guide were useful for quoting?
- Which assumptions had to change after measuring?
- Which drawings still required trade or engineering release?
- What did the full-size mockup prevent?
- Did the guide reduce missing information or simply move questions earlier?

### One specialty trade

Preferred: glazier or millworker, because the arches, ceiling mirror, bar, and desk make the handoff visible.

- What can a visualization specify, and what must a fabricator determine?
- Which template, load, clearance, or service detail controlled the build?
- What evidence should a future AI-assisted project collect sooner?

### Editor's rule for interviews

Ask for concrete examples. Cut generic praise. Never ask a trade to endorse Astra. Let the artifact and the corrections establish usefulness.

## Capture plan from preproduction to reveal

### Capture immediately, before the room changes

- Lock one 16:9 master camera position that can be recreated after the build. Mark tripod feet, lens height, focal length, tilt, time of day, and lighting state.
- Photograph all eight corners, the booth connection, both raised storage-door areas, ceiling, services, existing desk, cameras, speakers, lights, and door swings.
- Record a slow room walkthrough in both directions with clean natural sound.
- Film Greg using the room as it exists: rehearsal, setup, console work, and the awkward physical constraints he wants to solve.
- Record Greg's first interview in the current room.
- Screen-record the current guide, early 3D evidence, approved concept, live 3D, visualization, product records, and PDF packet at native resolution.
- Preserve the version and date in each screen-recording filename.

### Field survey and mockup

- Film laser and tape measurements with the value, datum, and location visible in the same shot.
- Capture M-01 through M-16 being completed, including both raised platforms.
- Film full-size furniture, appliance-door, chair-swivel, instrument-removal, and service envelopes taped on the floor.
- Record each discrepancy between plan, model, and field.
- Interview Greg and the survey lead beside the marked-up plan.

### Trade development

- Film sample reviews for antique mirror, finishes, fabric, slats, light temperature, rug, and furniture upholstery.
- Capture shop drawings, redlines, load and attachment decisions, appliance cutouts, and lighting controls.
- Film the point when a custom item is released and show the signed or dated evidence.
- Record quote and invoice totals only after they are final and approved for publication.

### Construction

- Capture room protection and the retained-equipment move log before removal.
- Record a 10 to 20 second fixed-angle clip at each chosen milestone. Use an unattended timelapse only if existing TRC equipment, power, storage, and site permission already support it.
- Film concealed conditions before closure: backing, wiring, drivers, ventilation, supports, and cable paths.
- Get one wide, one medium, and one detail for every major scope.
- Record the real correction whenever site conditions force a design change.
- Capture inspection, testing, punch list, and as-built labels.

### Furniture, systems, and styling

- Film delivery-route checks, assembly, final placement, and the first occupied layout test.
- Capture piano, desk, rack, TV, instruments, bar, seating, rug, and booth setup separately.
- Record lighting scenes, audio noise checks, service access, door swings, refrigerator operation, and AV cold start.
- Keep brand labels and serial numbers out of beauty shots unless cleared.

### Reveal

- Recreate the locked before angle before any people enter.
- Record a clean room pass with practical lighting, then a normal working-light pass.
- Capture Greg's first unscripted walkthrough and reaction.
- Repeat the field-of-view of the approved concept where physically possible, then show the differences without softening them.
- Photograph and film `before / concept / live 3D / visualization / installed result` for the final comparison.
- Record final costs, schedule, changes, and lessons only after the records close.

## Audio and visual treatment

### Picture

- Open on imperfect evidence, not the polished concept.
- Use hard cuts between versions when orientation, scale, or product shape changes.
- Put concept, 3D, visualization, and physical footage in the same frame only when each has a fixed label.
- Animate record lines, dimensions, and source paths as editorial annotations. Avoid generic particles, glowing AI interfaces, or fake terminal typing.
- Use warm brass for active evidence and red pencil only for a correction. Green appears only after a fact is field verified.
- Keep the real room's texture and color. Do not grade the before footage to look deliberately worse.
- Hold on failures long enough for viewers to inspect them.

### Sound

- Build the rhythm from real room sounds: cable clicks, keys, drum hits, tape pulls, laser beeps, pencil marks, saws, drivers, and relay clicks.
- Use a restrained instrumental score with tension in the early model sequence and space under interviews.
- Let the score drop out when Greg says, “you still have to do better.”
- Keep interface sounds sparse and source-based.
- Final mix targets: dialogue-led web mix, clean mono compatibility, and full captions. Keep music and effects on separate edit tracks if music is used.

### Graphics

- Timecode and filenames use IBM Plex Mono.
- Chapter titles use Barlow Condensed in large, narrow blocks.
- Evidence labels use five fixed terms: `RECORD`, `MODEL`, `VISUALIZATION`, `FIELD VERIFIED`, `PENDING`.
- Every number on screen cites a version or dated source in the lower frame.
- Quotes preserve Greg's original spelling and punctuation when shown as prompt evidence.

## Production approach

### In-house crew and equipment

- One paid TRC studio assistant captures short milestone clips with existing TRC cameras or a current phone, tripod, and microphones.
- One paid editor assembles the 5:30 hero, proof labels, captions, and 30/60 second cutdowns. The editor may be a qualified TRC staff member.
- Greg gives interviews, approves claims, and reviews one rough cut and one final cut.
- Astra prepares the evidence map, shot prompts, transcript, select suggestions, caption draft, proof labels, and factual export checklist inside the existing account and infrastructure.
- No new camera, phone, microphone, light, computer, or rental package is included.
- No outside producer, director of photography, sound recordist, motion designer, colorist, mixer, or agency is included.

### Paid hours are capped at 24

Capture is **8 paid hours** broken across the real construction calendar:

- Before-state room, Greg interview, and matched camera record: 2 hours.
- Field survey and floor mockup: 1 hour.
- Four short trade or construction milestones: 2 hours total, about 30 minutes each.
- Final installation, matched reveal, and Greg reaction: 2 hours.
- Native screen recordings, file labels, and handoff to edit: 1 hour.

Editing is **16 paid hours**:

- Ingest, organize, sync, and create the edit project: 2 hours.
- Assemble the 5:30 story from the approved outline and Astra selects: 6 hours.
- Add fixed evidence labels, simple comparisons, captions, and source notes: 4 hours.
- Complete one consolidated revision round: 2 hours.
- Export the hero plus 30 and 60 second cutdowns: 2 hours.

Greg contributes an estimated **4 to 6 hours** for interviews, decisions, and two review passes. This is owner time, not paid video labor. Astra work uses the existing service and is not counted as free contractor labor.

### Incremental cash allowance inside the $25,000 project cap

This is a planning allowance, not spending authorization. It must fit inside the room project's hard $25,000 all-in cap.

| Line | Assumption | Allowance |
|---|---|---:|
| Studio assistant capture | 8 paid hours at $25 per hour | **$200** |
| Editing support | 16 paid hours at $40 per hour | **$640** |
| Storage or music-rights contingency | Spend only if existing TRC storage or a cleared TRC-owned track cannot cover delivery | **$160** |
| **Maximum incremental cash** | 24 paid hours, existing equipment | **$1,000** |

Use a TRC-owned track only after written rights and contributor clearance are confirmed. If that clearance is unavailable, use no music or a properly documented zero-cost source. Do not assume a generic license. Any unspent contingency stays in the room budget.

The 24-hour plan depends on a locked outline, clean file naming, short milestone clips, one Greg interview setup at each end of the build, simple proof labels, and one consolidated revision. Added shoot days, complex animation, audio repair, multiple revision rounds, or a separate 90-second edit require a new estimate and cannot silently exceed the $1,000 ceiling.

These are campaign targets, not achieved results.

- A cold viewer can explain the story as “Greg kept correcting Astra until the room became a traceable build plan, then professionals verified and built it.”
- At least 70 percent of test viewers distinguish concept, 3D model, visualization, and installed room without explanation.
- At least 50 percent of viewers remain at 30 seconds on the hero film.
- At least 35 percent complete the 5:30 hero film.
- The project page earns a 2.5 percent or better click-through rate to the guide.
- The release generates 10 qualified conversations with studio owners, builders, designers, or AI teams in the first 60 days.
