# Build completeness audit v1.5

## Finding

The v1.4 guide is a strong **coordination and release-gate package**, but it is not yet a complete materials list or a set of full installation instructions. The current data contains 37 scope items, 44 sequenced tasks, seven coordinated drawings and 14 product references. Of the 14 products, only the sofa and lounge chair are selected references; nine remain candidates, two are owned-item checks, and the TV is only a dimensional reference. `research/products.json` is therefore a source register, not an orderable bill of materials: most rows have no purchase quantity, accessory kit, delivered finish, price, lead time or install assignment.

All seven drawings say **FIELD RELEASE REQUIRED**. A01 is a planning layout, A02–A05 are elevations/plans, E01 is lighting coordination, and D01 explicitly says it is not an anchor/load detail. The 44 tasks correctly tell trades what to survey, coordinate, install and test, but phrases such as “install the approved” assume later shop drawings, schedules and submittals that do not exist yet.

## Builder-stopping gaps in priority order

1. **No signed field record or revised issued set.** M-01 through M-16 are legitimate gates, especially structure/backing, circuits, equipment identities, occupied furniture fit, TV/instrument template, acoustic baseline, delivery route and raised platforms. Custom dimensions and final quantities cannot be responsibly released before these checks.
2. **No structural/glazing attachment package.** The overhead mirror, five arches, TV, instrument hangers, track, slat panels and cloud have no substrate-specific fasteners, loads, backing or inspection details. P1-T6 asks for this future work; it is not present in D01.
3. **No electrical and lighting construction set.** E01 has fixture points, but there is no circuit/control/driver/feed schedule, track system, zone riser, compatible dimmer decision, low-voltage path or service-access plan.
4. **No released millwork shop drawings.** Bar, counter, glass front, shelves, desk and rack remain envelopes. Appliance, ventilation, stone support, shelf load, rack heat and cable access are not fully detailed.
5. **No finish-system decisions.** Wall fabric versus solid finish, slat termination, dark floor versus retained oak, booth floor, paint products, trim and raised-platform treatment remain open. These decisions control demolition, takeoff and sequencing.
6. **No AV/cable/rack package.** The guide has strong functional acceptance tests but no rack elevation, cable schedule, connector/length quantities, patch map, pathway details, rack power or installation termination standard.
7. **No complete booth treatment package.** Absorbers, cloud, flooring and lights are provisional; glass sliders and retained equipment lack condition/service records.
8. **Several visible purchases have no product at all.** Three stools, cocktail table, rug/pad, engineer chair, piano/Korg seating and stands, TV/mount, track/head set, booth flooring/treatments and styling items cannot be ordered from the current guide.

## What the next complete release must contain

- One order ledger keyed to every guide ID with classification, maker, model/SKU, exact finish, approved quantity, accessory/fastener kit, current source, lead time, delivered location, responsible buyer and release revision.
- Field-updated A01–A05/E01 plus substrate-specific attachment sheets, millwork shop drawings, finish/floor/platform details, a lighting circuit/control/driver schedule, and AV rack/signal/cable/pathway drawings.
- Trade installation packets that state preparation, substrates, tolerances, fastening or connection method, work sequence, interfaces, protection, inspection hold points, testing, cleaning, spares and as-built evidence. Manufacturer manuals should be attached for every selected off-the-shelf item.
- A retained-equipment ledger for every layout-only ID so “owned” is proven by model plate, dimensions, condition, destination, required stand/bench/mount/cable and move/setup instruction.

The existing field gates should remain. They are the correct way to avoid invented engineering. Completion means converting their results into issued purchase schedules and trade instructions; “contractor verify” by itself does not release work.
